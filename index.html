01 研究概要

Research Statement

My work asks one question in two settings: when a model produces something, what handle does a person get on how it got there — and how do they change it?

On the interaction side, Re-Think restructures LLM inference into two stages with an editable intermediate. The model surfaces its own clarifying question–answer pairs before answering; the user reads them, corrects what is wrong, and the model reasons again from the corrected trace. Across reasoning benchmarks and user studies this improved accuracy, calibration of trust, and engagement — reasoning becomes a shared artefact rather than a black box.

On the modeling side, I work on generation under explicit structural control. My BLP 2025 paper conditions Bangla poetry generation on rhyme, meter, word-count and boundary tokens, so a model reaches a requested poetic form instead of approximating it — a hard case, because Bangla is low-resource and its prosody is not well served by architectures tuned on English.

These are the same problem from two directions: legible, steerable model behaviour. I want to continue it in a lab that takes the modeling and the human side equally seriously.

I work on controllable generation and language modeling for low-resource settings — making a model hit an explicitly specified target rather than approximating it.

My BLP 2025 paper introduces a structure-aware framework for Bangla poetry generation: fine-tuned LLMs conditioned on rhyme, meter, word-count and boundary control tokens, with variable structural conditioning that permits flexible poetic forms. It improved rhyme consistency, metrical balance and coherence in a setting where data is scarce and prosody differs sharply from the English corpora most architectures are tuned on.

Re-Think extends the same instinct to inference: a two-stage procedure that exposes the model's intermediate reasoning as editable question–answer pairs, so a correction to the trace propagates into the final answer. It is a decoding-time control mechanism as much as an interface, and it improved accuracy across several reasoning benchmarks.

I am looking for a lab where I can push further on controlled generation, multilingual and low-resource NLP, and inference-time methods for steering model behaviour.

I work on human–AI interaction — specifically, on giving people a real handle on machine reasoning instead of a verdict to accept or reject.

Re-Think, my current project, restructures LLM inference into two stages joined by an editable intermediate. Rather than emitting a chain of thought as a read-only artefact, the model surfaces its own clarifying question–answer pairs; the user inspects them, edits what is wrong, and the model reasons again from the corrected trace. Evaluation combined reasoning benchmarks with user studies, and showed gains in accuracy alongside improvements in user trust and engagement — the metacognitive loop matters as much as the score.

This grew out of a broader interest in metacognition in AI and in user-centered design for systems whose behaviour is probabilistic. My modeling work on structurally-controlled generation is the same commitment approached from the other side: designing the control surface, not just the output.

I want to pursue this in a lab working on interaction design for intelligent systems, mixed-methods evaluation, or human-centered AI.
